# Dovetail-split STL generation — feasibility report

**Bead 11 deliverable. Investigation only; no pipeline changes proposed yet.**

## Executive summary

Baking dovetail splits into the STL pipeline is **feasible and recommended (yes-with-caveats)**.
A boolean-cut PoC (`bin/poc_dovetail_split.py`) splits a 100K-face Japan STL into
two interlocking watertight pieces in **~90 ms** end-to-end on trimesh+manifold3d,
scaling to **~3.5 s** at 3 M faces. The dovetail geometry is straightforward
(one trapezoidal tab centred on a cut plane), the existing `manifold_clean`
pattern from `make_all_sa_with_vector_clip.py` handles the post-boolean cleanup
unchanged, and BambuStudio's joint parameters (Depth / Width / Flap-angle /
Groove-angle) map 1:1 onto our PoC parameters. The load-bearing caveats are
**joint-orientation for FDM layer adhesion** and **multi-cut recursion for
Russia/Antarctica-scale meshes** — neither blocking, both worth their own
design pass in the implementation bead. Estimated effort: **3–5 engineer-days**.

## BambuStudio joint reference

BambuStudio's "Cut" tool offers four connector styles; the **Dovetail
(Groove)** connector is the relevant one. Source lives in
`src/slic3r/GUI/Gizmos/GLGizmoAdvancedCut.{hpp,cpp}` (verified via web
search; sparse-clone of the repo confirmed the file path). The exposed
parameters in the GUI are:

| Param         | Meaning                                                          | Our PoC equivalent       |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Depth         | How far the tab protrudes from the cut plane                     | `tab_depth_mm` (15 mm)   |
| Width         | Tab width at the **base** (the cut plane)                        | `tab_base_mm` (30 mm)    |
| Flap angle    | Outward angle of the tab sides → wider at the tip than the base  | derived from base vs tip |
| Groove angle  | Inward angle of the slot sides (matches flap for friction fit)   | symmetric in our PoC     |

Shape: an isosceles trapezoid whose tip edge is **longer** than the base
edge, sitting in the cut plane and extruded through the full mesh height.
This is the mechanically classical sliding-dovetail joint — the two halves
must be assembled by sliding along the joint normal (the Z axis, for our
flat-on-bed prints), and once assembled they cannot pull apart in the
print plane. The "flap angle" controls how aggressively the trapezoid
flares; ~10–15° is typical (matches our PoC's 30→45 mm widening over
15 mm depth ⇒ ~26° per side, on the loose end of typical and a deliberate
choice for the friction-fit forgiveness FDM needs).

The slicer-side joint is generated by booleaning a 3D prism of that
trapezoid through the model and writing the two cut halves out as
separate parts in the project file; the implementation is conventional
CSG, not anything proprietary or hard to replicate.

## Findings by investigation area

### 1. Joint geometry (replicable, yes)

Replicating Bambu's joint shape is trivial: a single trapezoidal extrusion
plus a clearance offset. Our PoC builds the splitter outline as a
half-plane rectangle with a trapezoidal bump on the cut edge (8-vertex
shapely polygon, see `build_splitter_solid()`). The slot piece subtracts
a **slightly enlarged** version of the same prism (`clearance_mm` = 0.20
mm per side by default) so a 0.4 mm total gap accommodates FDM print
tolerance. We do **not** need to match Bambu's joint pixel-for-pixel —
the user's existing fit-tolerance numbers (from the South America print
tests, ~0.3 mm at scale 0.33) tell us the joint shape that lives in the
STL only has to fit itself, not Bambu's joint.

### 2. Cut-plane choice (axis-aligned midline is the right default)

The PoC splits at the bbox midline of whichever axis is longer (X or Y).
For one cut this is fine; for Russia (needs ~12 pieces at scale 0.33) the
implementation will need **recursive halving**: while any sub-piece exceeds
the bed (220 mm at the configured scale), split it again along its own
longer axis. This gives a balanced grid (Russia → roughly 4×3 = 12 pieces)
with no human input. Two refinements the report recommends for follow-up,
not for the first implementation:
* **Capital avoidance**: nudge the cut line ±5 mm to keep the capital
  star whole inside one piece. Cheap; lookup-table from CAPITALS.
* **User override** per group, e.g. `group.split_plan: {"USA":
  [("x", "-100°")]}`. Politically useful (split USA at the Continental
  Divide, not through Chicago). Trivial to wire in once auto-split works.

### 3. Construction approach (boolean cut + add, single splitter prism)

The PoC uses approach **(b) "interlocking plane sweep"** — one prism
that doubles as the cut plane AND the dovetail. This is strictly simpler
than approach (a) (cut flat, then subtract slot from one piece, union
tab onto the other) — half the boolean calls, half the chances of
manifold3d returning a non-volume. The trimesh+manifold3d pipeline
already used for capital-star cutting works without modification: pass
through `manifold_clean()` after each boolean, and the STL roundtrip
stays watertight. **No new dependencies.**

### 4. Joint-parameter scaling (proportional to cut length, capped)

Fixed joint sizes don't scale. A 30 mm-wide tab is appropriate for a
150–250 mm cut line, but undersized for a 600 mm Russia-cut and
overpowered for a 60 mm Sri Lanka split. The recommendation:
* Tab base width = max(15 mm, min(50 mm, 0.15 × cut_length_mm))
* Tab tip width = 1.5 × base (≈ 27° flap angle, matches PoC)
* Tab depth = 0.5 × base
* Clearance = 0.20 mm (fixed; this is a printer-tolerance constant, not
  a geometry-scale constant)
For very long cuts (> 300 mm), consider **two tabs** placed at the 1/3
and 2/3 marks rather than one giant tab — better registration, prevents
the cut from racking. Out-of-scope for the first cut but the geometry
generalises naturally (the splitter outline gains four more vertices per
extra tab).

### 5. Print orientation (caveat — must verify by physical test)

This is the **one finding that needs hardware validation** before the
implementation bead ships. The pipeline prints with Z = print-bed-normal,
so the dovetail's load axis (the direction in which the tab resists
being pulled out) lies in the XY plane — i.e. **parallel to the FDM
layer lines**. That's the wrong orientation: pulling forces will
delaminate along a layer line rather than fracture across the tab. Two
mitigations:
* **Beef up the tab** (depth ≥ 15 mm, base ≥ 30 mm) so even an
  inter-layer separation has enough friction surface to hold.
* **Add a small Z-direction "key"** — a 1 mm vertical bump at the tab
  centreline that mates into a matching dimple in the slot. This locks
  the joint against Z-axis separation, which is the load direction the
  layer lines actually resist well.
The PoC ships without the Z-key (single trapezoidal tab only); a
print test on the synthetic 400×200×5 slab will tell us whether the
beefed-up tab alone is enough, or whether the Z-key is mandatory. This
is the **single load-bearing unknown** in the complexity estimate below.

### 6. Computational cost (cheap; not a bottleneck)

Measured on this box (single-threaded trimesh + manifold3d):

| Mesh size       | Boolean cost (intersect + difference + clean) |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 12 faces        | < 1 ms                                        |
| 100 K faces     | ~90 ms (Japan_solid.stl, real production STL) |
| 200 K faces     | ~210 ms                                       |
| 3 M faces       | ~3.5 s                                        |

Production country STLs are 50 K – 100 K faces post-simplification, so a
single cut adds ~0.1 s. Russia's 12-piece recursive split runs ~11 cuts
end-to-end — call it 5 s of boolean work on top of the existing
country generation cost (which is minutes). **Negligible.** Do the
split **after** simplification, not before — the unsimplified meshes
are 1–3 M faces (~3 s per cut, 30 s for Russia) and the joint geometry
gets pointlessly fine on the unsimplified surface.

### 7. UX / configuration (per-group YAML + driver flag)

Recommendation: split intent lives in **two places**.
* **CountryGroup config** (where every other per-group setting lives —
  `groups.py`): a `split: "auto" | "none" | {"axis": "x", "coord": …}`
  field. Default: `"auto"` — recurse until every piece fits the bed.
  `"none"` for groups whose largest country fits.
* **Driver flag**: `--max-bed-mm 220` override on `make_country_group.py`
  so it can target different printers without rewriting the config.
Output naming convention: replace
`STLs/Russia/<ts>/Russia_solid.stl` with
`STLs/Russia/<ts>/Russia_solid_NW.stl`, `…_NE.stl`, etc., **and** record
the split plan in `alignment.json` so the assembly QC tooling
(`stl_align_gui.py`) can find related pieces.

## Recommended implementation approach

Single-prism cut (PoC approach), auto-recursive halving until each piece
fits the print bed, executed **after** the existing simplification step
and **before** the capital-star cut. Reuse `manifold_clean()` verbatim.
Per-group `split` config in `groups.py`, default `"auto"`, with the
recursive halving function living in a new module
`split_pipeline.py` (rough function names: `plan_splits(mesh, bed_mm)`,
`apply_split_plan(mesh, plan)`, `dovetail_splitter(bounds, axis, coord,
params)`). Capital-star post-step must check which piece the capital
ends up in. Joint params scale with cut length per §4.

## Complexity estimate

**3–5 engineer-days**, broken out:
* Recursive halving + bed-fit logic: 0.5 d
* Splitter geometry, params, scaling: 0.5 d (PoC already 80% there)
* Pipeline integration in `make_country_group.py` + driver flag: 0.5–1 d
* Capital-star "which piece am I on" handling: 0.5 d
* `alignment.json` schema update + `stl_align_gui.py` awareness: 0.5 d
* Tests + print-test iteration (the **only** open-ended chunk): 1–2 d

Load-bearing unknowns that move the upper bound:
1. **FDM layer-line orientation** — if the trapezoidal tab alone is not
   strong enough and a Z-key is required, add 0.5 d for the keying
   geometry and another print-test cycle.
2. **Manifold3d robustness on coastline-degenerate cuts** — if a cut
   plane that grazes a tiny offshore island crashes the boolean, add up
   to 1 d for a "nudge the cut to avoid sub-N km² polygons" pre-check.
   The Japan PoC didn't hit this, but Japan has clean islands; the
   Philippines or Indonesia could.

## UX recommendation

Per-group config in `groups.py`, default `split: "auto"`. Override path
for politically-sensitive cuts (USA at the Continental Divide). Driver
flag `--max-bed-mm` for printer agnosticism. Output filenames suffixed
with quadrant labels (`_NW`, `_NE`, …) and split metadata recorded in
`alignment.json` alongside the existing per-country pose data.

## Risks and open questions

1. **Print-test required before merging.** Computational watertightness
   is necessary but not sufficient (per `CLAUDE.md`'s GOLD_STLs rule).
   The implementation bead must include a physical print of the
   synthetic slab joint AND of one real country (suggest USA, which the
   user has already split manually in Bambu — direct A/B against the
   Bambu-cut version).
2. **Z-key needed?** Open. Single-tab dovetail may delaminate under
   pull-out load given XY-plane joint axis vs Z-axis layer lines. The
   PoC does not include a Z-key. Determine empirically.
3. **Three-piece junctions** (Russia at 4×3 split) — out of scope per
   the bead, but the recursive halving plan produces them implicitly.
   Each cell will need its joints generated independently from each
   neighbour; the splitter prism approach generalises fine (one
   splitter per cut, applied sequentially) but ordering matters and
   should be made deterministic.
4. **alignment.json schema migration**: add a `split_plan` field per
   group, and add per-piece pose entries. Backwards-compatible (older
   readers ignore unknown fields).
5. **Capital-on-cut-line**: if the auto-midline runs through the
   capital city, the star geometry collides with the joint. Detect and
   nudge ≥ 1.5× star radius away. Cheap.
6. **Holes (lakes, fjords) intersecting the cut**: the splitter prism is
   solid through the full Z range, so a lake hole in the original mesh
   stays a hole in both pieces. Verified by inspection of the Japan PoC
   output (no degenerate faces). Not a real risk; mentioned for
   completeness.
